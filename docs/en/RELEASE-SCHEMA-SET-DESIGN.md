The schemas stay **DRAFT**; the open points P1, P2, P4, P7 and Forms #167 are listed with their dependencies in `docs/en/RELEASE-SCHEMA-OPEN-POINTS.md`. Statements below that say "D1 blocks finalization" are the history of the #38 state; D1 never was the only blocker.

Status: **design draft, documentation and evidence only.** The schema parts in `docs/design/release-schemas/` are **drafts and are not finalized**: finalization is on hold until the grammar conformance of the node name `createdBy` is settled (decision D1, section 6). Not part of this pull request: the derived JSON Schema, any lint, any compiler or verifier refactor, any change of the Form, any change of the grammar.

Principle in force: **the current capabilities of a consumer must not rewrite the meaning of the normative contract.** The schemas say what must hold, whatever the Form can evaluate today.

## 2. The four layers

| Layer | Question | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Normative schema | what must hold? | `common.yaml`, `primitive-release.yaml`, `module-release.yaml` (CIC grammar), composed by `composition.yaml` |
| Form | what can it evaluate? | measured: `docs/en/RELEASE-GRAMMAR-CAPABILITY-MEASUREMENT.md`, the probe of this pull request |
| Verifier | which further checks does it make? | the reason identifiers of `docs/en/RELEASE-RECORD-CONTRACT.md` section 11 and the generalization plan |
| Evidence | what shows the checks ran? | the conformance vectors (planned) and the declaration check |

A later Form that evaluates contracts changes a status in the declaration, never a schema.

## 3. The parts and their composition

* **`common.yaml`**: the members every record kind has: `kind`, `version`, `timestamp`, `validity`, `createdBy`, `provenance` (`envelope`, `source_commit`), `artifacts` (`manifest_digest`, `files[]` with `path` as the list key and `digest`), `release` (`envelope`, `createdBy`, `build_hash`, `sign`), and `cic_countersign`. `createdBy` and `validity` are common (decision O1). `cic_countersign` is **structurally optional** (`optional: true`): that a counter-signature is required for acceptance is the admission policy, not the schema.
* **`primitive-release.yaml`**: adds `provenance.{dependency_lock_sha256, grammar_sha256, grammar_schema_sha256}` (as in the existing schema, a string or null) and `specs[]` (`id`, `source_path` as the key, `meta_hash`, an opaque `spec`, at least one). A surface-level `must` contract states `kind = 'PrimitiveRelease'`.
* **`module-release.yaml`**: one member of its own, **`name`** (mandatory string; review #38 P5.1: ModuleRelease-specific, part of the signed identity of the release, not in the common envelope, absent from PrimitiveRelease records, whose published shape has no top-level `name`). The shipped descriptor and the WASM bytes are bound through `artifacts.files`, with **no duplicate pointer members** (P5.2). Three surface-level `must` contracts: the kind, and exactly one `artifacts/files` entry for `module/module.wasm` and for `project.yaml`. A `specs` member or a primitive provenance field is **unknown** to it and refused; likewise a `name` in a PrimitiveRelease record is unknown to that schema.
* **Composition** (`composition.yaml`, `compose.py`; the rules, not yet a production tool): a kind part **adds** members and never removes, relaxes or redefines a common one; a composite with the same name at the same path is merged by adding the kind's fields **only if every other attribute of the two composites is identical** (`mandatory`, `optional`, `nullable`, `contract`, ...; the kind part restates them, so nothing can be relaxed, added or dropped silently); a duplicate field name inside one part, a kind-part member outside `spec.identity` / `spec.config_surface`, an unknown `config_surface` key and a different `aggregate_ref` are errors too; every other overlap is a collision, a composition-time error `schema_field_collision`, never a verification result; the surface-level `validation` entries are concatenated. The kind constant is declared by the kind part, not by the common part.
* Every part is itself a valid composition instance; the composed schema of each kind is generated, never edited.

## 4. Contract declarations and the conformance declaration

The parts declare **21** `contract` entries: 17 on nodes (patterns for `version`, `timestamp`, the dates, the commit, the digests, the hashes and the signatures; a `range` `3..3` for the two envelope numbers; an `enum` for `signed_payload`) and 4 at the surface (`must`). Patterns use only a plain syntax (no look-around), because the regular-expression flavor of the `pattern` type is not specified by the grammar (open point, section 7). Rules that a pattern could not carry stay named verifier checks (`artifact_path_form`, `artifact_order`, the recomputations, the signatures, the admission-profile checks), listed in `verifier_only`; none of them is a contract entry.

`contract-conformance.yaml` declares, for every contract entry: its place, type and expression; the **Form status** (`unevaluated` for node entries, `not_reported` for surface entries); the **verifier status** with the reasons that cover it; and the planned vector class. **Every normative requirement has a named owner and an explicit status**: `implemented` (the reference verifier makes the check today: the reason is in the contract's section 11 table or the blob layer), `planned` (named by the generalization plan, no code), `implemented_draft` (the draft composer), or `not_implemented` (no executor; it needs a note, no reasons). The declaration also lists `further_requirements` (V01-V09 and P01: rules a schema cannot express, such as the path form, the order, the recomputations, the blob layer, and the two ModuleRelease decisions of P5) and `global_requirements` (G01-G04): G01 interpretation identity, **G02 the closed value domain with the 2^53-1 integer bound over the whole value tree, opaque member contents included (not only the declared integer fields)**, G03 the pre-tree rules, G04 the composition rule. `check_declaration.py` checks: (1) every contract of the parts is declared exactly once and the reverse (a duplicate in the parts is an error, not an overwrite); (2) the parts carry no evaluation status; (3) the Form status against the probe output (17 and 13 listed nodes for the two kinds, no surface entry listed); (4) **executor ownership (review F2.1)**: every requirement has an explicit `owner` from a closed set (`verifier`, `producer`, `composition`; `none` only with the status `not_implemented`, and a `not_implemented` entry has owner `none`, a note and no reasons, so it can never count as an executed check); a missing, empty or unknown owner is an error; the owner must fit the kind of reason (`schema_field_collision` is executed by the composition, `declaration_required_path_missing` by the producer, every other reason by the verifier); every requirement has an allowed status, `implemented` only for reasons the reference verifier makes, `planned` only for plan reasons, and an `implemented` or `planned` entry has reasons; (5) **inventory accounting**: each of the 38 known reason identifiers (the contract table, the blob layer, the plan's kind, composition and admission-profile reasons, the producer reason of the module release plan, and the module identity reason proposed by this draft) has at least one owner in the declaration, so removing an owner is an error, and G01-G03 and the 2^53-1 statement of G02 are present. `selftest_declaration.py` (50 checks) accepts the real set and refuses 48 planted defects, each with its own reason (38 in the declaration and the probe evidence, 7 of them the P7 cases: the producer or the verifier executor of `integer_out_of_range` removed, the independence statement removed, a wrong owner; removed contracts and requirements, 13 owner and status cases such as a removed, empty, unknown, mismatched or `none` owner and a not_implemented entry that still names an executor, weakened G02, a duplicated contract; 10 in the composition: collisions, attribute conflicts, duplicate names, an unknown key, a different aggregate_ref), and shows a kind part adding a field to `provenance` being merged.

## 5. Evidence

* **Grammar conformance**: the pinned grammar's own checker (`check_grammar.py`, self-test 53/53) accepts `primitive-release.yaml` and `module-release.yaml` and finds in `common.yaml` and in both composed schemas **exactly three findings, all the node name `createdBy`**. Every other construct of the drafts (the contracts, `optional`, the list key, the opaque field, the surface-level `validation`, an empty `nodes` list in the module part) conforms to the grammar.
* **Form probe** (Forms `2f02a75`, profile `cic-primitives-73ae469-6a188078`; Rust and Go byte-identical; 4 cases, 30 documents, all accounted for): both composed schemas compile. Every conforming document is accepted: the sanitized published `v0.2.1` shape, one without the counter-signature, a `ModuleRelease` record. Every structural defect is refused: a missing `source_commit`, a missing `specs`, a missing grammar digest, an unknown top-level or provenance field, a duplicate artifact path (`PATH_DUPLICATE_IDENTITY`), an empty `files`, an envelope written as a string, a counter-signature without its authority, a `specs` member or a primitive provenance field on a `ModuleRelease`, a missing `kind`, a primitive record against the module schema and the reverse. The kind parts therefore do not mix.
* **What the Form does not evaluate** (recorded, `inf_` documents, accepted): a bad `version`, a bad `source_commit`, a `kind` of `Other`, a bad digest form, an envelope `4`, unsorted artifacts, a `ModuleRelease` that names the wrong kind or lacks `module/module.wasm` or `project.yaml`. These are exactly the contract entries and verifier checks of the declaration.
* **New finding, not decided here**: the Form lists the 17 node-level contracts as unevaluated but **neither evaluates nor lists the 4 surface-level `validation` entries** (the `must` contracts of the kind parts): it drops them silently. It is the same kind of finding as FG1 (a declared construct that has no visible effect) and belongs to the Forms. Review decision on #38: the native declarations stay in the draft; a separate Forms issue is opened (draft prepared, `ISSUE-DRAFT-forms-surface-validation`); **finalization of the schemas stays on hold until the responsibility and the visibility of the surface-level checks are settled**; no local hidden second validator is added to work around it. Until then the declaration lists these four entries as `not_reported`, and each has a named verifier executor (`kind_missing`, `kind_unsupported`, `kind_schema_violation`) or is `planned`.

## 6. D1: preparing the grammar change (not made here)

The pinned grammar limits a node name to `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`; the records `v0.1.5`, `v0.2.0` and `v0.2.1` have `createdBy` (at `/createdBy` and `/release/createdBy`). By the decision, the rule is changed in the grammar, as a separate issue and pull request, through a **versioned grammar profile**; the Form gets no `createdBy` exception; the records, the field names and the hash input stay as they are. What the preparation fixes, before the issue is opened:

* the change is to the **general** name rule, not to one name; which new rule (for example admitting an upper-case letter after the first character) is the question of the grammar issue, with its consequences for every existing schema and for the Forms' pinned profile;
* the Forms carry the profile identifier `cic-primitives-73ae469-6a188078`; a new profile is a Forms work package of its own (the pin work package), not part of this one;
* until the rule is settled, `check_grammar.py` reports the three `createdBy` findings and is the **finalization gate** of the schemas: the drafts are final only when it reports none.

A draft of the grammar issue is prepared (not opened).

**Settled 2026-11-21:** the gate above is met (V2: 0 findings on both composed schemas; V1: exactly the three `createdBy` findings; evidence `docs/evidence/release-schema-d1-gate-closed/`). Meeting this gate proves grammatical conformance only; it does not finalize the schemas (see `docs/en/RELEASE-SCHEMA-OPEN-POINTS.md`).

## 7. Open points

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| P1 | The regular-expression flavor of the `pattern` contract is unspecified. **Review decision:** a versioned grammar decision with Rust-Go-Python conformance vectors; the patterns are not final before it. |
| P2 | The expression dialect of the `must` contract. **Review decision:** a separate grammar specification; no implicit XPath/JPath mixture. The draft expressions are placeholders until then. |
| P3 | Where the composition rule lives. **Review decision:** it is defined in the release contract; the composer only executes it. |
| P4 | `timestamp` pattern stricter than `format: date-time`. **Review decision:** not tightened silently; a separate A-profile decision. The draft pattern (C02) is marked provisional by this point. |
| P5 | The ModuleRelease part. **Review decision:** the `artifacts.files` binding is enough, no redundant pointers; a ModuleRelease-specific mandatory `name` (P5.1); the two module artifacts exactly once, producer- and verifier-side (P5.2). Effect in the draft: see below. |
| P6 | Nullable provenance digests. **Review decision:** stays as the current contract says; tightening is a separate decision. |
| P7 | **Decided (owner, 2026-11-21): option B.** An integer outside -(2^53-1) .. +(2^53-1) is refused with `integer_out_of_range`; a value of another type with `value_type_unsupported`; the whole value tree including opaque members; by the producer and, independently, by the verifier; profile H unchanged. Recorded in the plan and the declaration (G02, P02); not implemented. `docs/en/RELEASE-P7-INTEGER-RANGE-REASON.md`. |

**P5 decisions (review #38) and their effect in the draft.** The module release plan (`CIC_Relay` `docs/en/concept/module-release-record-plan.md`, head `1bd2acd`, "Record members" and "Contract boundary") was compared with the draft: the common members, no `specs`, a per-kind schema sharing a common part agree. Two differences were found and are now decided:

* **P5.1, `name` stays (GO).** The plan's top-level `name` is kept as a **ModuleRelease-specific mandatory member** (`module-release.yaml`), never in the common envelope, never in PrimitiveRelease records (the Form refuses it there as an unknown field: probe `neg_module_name_in_primitive_record`). The verifier checks that it equals the identity of the **authenticated module descriptor** (requirement V08, `planned`, reason `module_identity_mismatch`, an identifier proposed by this draft that the plan does not yet name). The Form cannot evaluate this: a differing name is accepted (probe `inf_name_differs_from_descriptor_identity`), which is exactly why V08 names the verifier as the executor. `name` is not a plain artifact reference but part of the signed identity of the release.
* **P5.2, mandatory module artifacts (GO).** `module/module.wasm` and `project.yaml` must each appear **exactly once** in the authenticated artifact list; further artifacts are allowed; the producer refuses a declaration lacking either (P01, `declaration_required_path_missing`, producer-side, `planned`) **and** the verifier refuses a record lacking or repeating either (V09, `kind_schema_violation`, `planned`) **even when the record is cryptographically valid**: the signature proves the content, not the semantic completeness of the module release. The surface-level `must` contracts state it in the schema; they are not evaluated by the Form (F3), so the verifier is the named executor. No pointer members are duplicated in the envelope.

The draft's contract text for the `name` format is unspecified (no pattern is declared, none is invented).
