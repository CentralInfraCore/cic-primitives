# The T3 time-stamping: the confirmed resolutions and what must be verified before it may be performed

**A record, a state machine and a check: documentation and isolated tooling only.** It performs **nothing**: no time stamp is requested, no key used, nothing signed, uploaded, renewed or published. It keeps four things apart: the **decisions** (the requirements the owner confirmed on 2026-11-23), the **conditions**, each in a **phase** (before the GO, at the capture, at the preservation), the **GO** to perform the time-stamping, which is a separate owner record that can never be written in the prerequisites file, and the **verdict** of a state machine in which **no state is an authorisation**. Data: `docs/design/time-evidence/t3-execution-prerequisites.yaml`; check: `docs/evidence/time-execution-prereq/check_t3_readiness.py`; the manifest: `docs/en/T3-MANIFEST-CONTRACT.md`.

## 1. What the owner confirmed, and what that is not

The six resolutions were a **reviewer's recommendation of 2026-11-23**. **The owner confirmed all six in a statement of the same day** (the message headed "T3 - tulajdonosi döntés és korlátozott GO", recorded verbatim in the record as `owner_statement`), **Q6a with the order `staged`**. Three things stay true:

* **The confirmation is a recorded statement, not an authenticated one.** The check verifies its **form** (a kind, a date, a reference, a statement of its authenticity, the verbatim text) and that **every decision's status and the chosen order in the record agree with the statement**; it cannot verify who wrote it. Anyone who can edit the file could write the same words; authenticating the owner's decisions is a separate decision. The check says so in its output on every run.
* **A confirmed decision is not a fulfilled condition.** The requirements are what became binding; the conditions (section 3) are what must still be **shown to be true** with evidence, and none of them is satisfied by the decision.
* **The confirmation is not the GO.** The owner's statement gives **one limited authorisation only**: the **read-only research** of the two providers (`docs/en/T3-TSA-PROVIDER-RESEARCH.md`); its result is **not an automatic fulfilment of a condition**, and where the evidence is missing the condition stays `open`. **On hold:** building the storage, the time-stamping, any key use, merge, implementation, CI changes, normative publication, the G-0 closure, the upload of the factory logs. A closing pull request may be opened only after the documentation task is closed and finally reviewed, **only to obtain CI evidence, without a merge permission**.

## 3. The conditions, by phase

A condition is `open` until it names **evidence that the check can read**: a file of the repository and a text that the file must contain, with a date. A condition that names evidence the file does not hold, or an open condition that claims evidence, makes the record **invalid**. The **phase** says **when** the evidence can exist: `pre` before the GO; `capture` only after the GO, when the tokens exist (the manifest built at execution time, the verified raw tokens, their immediate placement in two locations); `preserve` the end state (the third copy, the final package, the restore drills, the renewal plan). **A capture or preserve condition can be `verified` only after a valid GO record exists, and a preserve condition only after the capture is complete**: the record never demands before the execution evidence that can only exist after it. A constraint is a standing rule that is satisfied by being written down. Under the **strict** order the conditions marked `strict` move from `preserve` to `pre`. **A condition that needs the tokens** (it can only be true once the RFC 3161 tokens exist: the manifest built at execution time, the verified raw tokens, the final package, the final restore drills) is **never** pre-execution, in **either** order, may not carry a `strict` marking, and its evidence must be **digest-bound** (`evidence.sha256`, checked against the file): a text match is enough for a reference, not for the final token and storage evidence (review T3-F6).

## 4. The state machine, and the result today

`NOT_PREPARED` (a decision is not confirmed, or a pre-execution condition is open) → `PREPARED_FOR_OWNER_GO` (every decision confirmed, every pre-execution condition verified with evidence the check reads, no later-phase evidence) → `GO_RECORDED` (a valid **separate** GO record binds the prepared state by its digest) → `EVIDENCE_CAPTURED` (every capture condition verified) → `PRESERVATION_COMPLETE` (every preserve condition verified). **None of these authorises anything.** `PREPARED_FOR_OWNER_GO` is **not** the GO; the GO is an owner act, recorded in `docs/design/time-evidence/t3-execution-go.yaml` (absent today) with the digest of the prepared state; **the GO binds three inputs**: the decisions, the pre-execution conditions **and the expected set of the 92 open records of the inventory** (identifier, repository, path, ref, commit); if any of them changes after the GO, the digest no longer matches and the record is invalid.

`python3 docs/evidence/time-execution-prereq/check_t3_readiness.py` (`results/check_t3_readiness.txt`, exit 1): **`T3 time-stamping: NOT_PREPARED`**, and **valid**: every decision is confirmed, and the record is not prepared **only because 11 pre-execution conditions are open** (the providers' terms, trust chains and access, the executor, the two storage locations with their read-back, the custody). Of the 26 conditions, **4 of the 15 pre-execution conditions are verified**, 0 of 4 capture, 0 of 7 preserve (the verified three: the manifest builder and verifier are tested, a dry run builds exactly the expected set, the standing rule for the template releases); all six decisions are `confirmed` (Q6a: `staged`).

## 5. The order of the preservation (Q6a): staged or strict

The target of resolution Q3 is **2026-11-30**, seven days after this was written. The preservation requires the storage decision S3 (a provider, an account, payment, a holder: S10), which is **still open**, and **nothing exists**. The irreversible, deadline-bound step is **obtaining the tokens**; the number of preserved copies can be raised later, provided the evidence does not get lost in between. Two orders:

* **Staged** (recommended by the review, with strict preconditions): **before the GO** at least **two separate, verified storage locations are prepared** (provisioned, with a dry-run package read back and its digest verified: not merely named); the two raw tokens and the certificate chains are placed in **both** immediately after the capture and **read back** (a placement that is not read back is not counted as a success); the three-copy preservation, the final package and the restore drills are complete by the internal latest date, 2026-12-15.
* **Strict**: all **three storage locations** are prepared and verified **before** the GO, with a **dry-run package (no real token)** read back from each and restore drills with that package passed; this puts the target date at risk. **In both orders the final package with the raw tokens exists only after the capture and is verified there** (C-CAP-3, C-PRES-1): a strict order never demands evidence that cannot exist before the GO, and the self-test completes the strict order in its real sequence (prepared, the GO, the capture, the preservation).

In **both** orders the final package, the final restore drills and the verification of the raw tokens are **after** the capture. **The owner chose the staged order** (2026-11-23). The verdict changes with the choice (the self-test shows that the same fixture is `PREPARED_FOR_OWNER_GO` under the staged order and `NOT_PREPARED` under the strict order while the strict conditions are open).

## 6. The preserved package and the preservation requirements (Q6)

* **Contents** (C-Q6-4): the record bytes, or a **digest-verified restorable source** (the git objects at the recorded commits, with the bundle that restores them); the canonical manifest and its digest; the **raw token of each authority** (not a summary); the TSA certificate chains and the independently obtained trust anchors; the **frozen version** of the verification procedure and of the tools that built and verified the manifest.
* **Copies**: at least three, in at least two independent failure domains: immutable object storage (versioning and compliance retention), a second independent provider, an offline or isolated archive. **Putting files in a bucket is not the evidence:** a restore drill from **each** copy with a cryptographic check (C-Q6-6) is.
* **Time:** a **planning minimum of 10 years**, with a restore and cryptographic check every year; the ten years are a design minimum, **not a statement of a legal obligation** of the CIC.
* **Renewal** (C-Q6-7): the hash algorithm and the TSA chains weaken with time; a mechanism to renew the evidence (re-time-stamping before the weakening) has to be **planned**; it is not implemented here.
* **Custody** (C-Q6-5): a named holder, separate credentials, recovery and a successor. Nothing is backed up today (the T3 storage documents: **NOT BACKED UP**).

## 7. The exact record set (the manifest)

The manifest has to hold **exactly** the expected records, not merely their number. The inventory records the **expected set** of open records (`open_records`: the identifier, the repository, the path, the ref and the commit of each of the 92); the check parses the dry-run manifest and compares the two **identifier by identifier**, with the commit and the ref: a record that is swapped for another is reported as missing and as extra although the count is right, and a record at another commit than the one the expected set pins is refused. (The SHA-256 of each record's bytes is a different check, `verify_manifest.py`, against git.) At execution the manifest is built from the then-current commits and compared with the then-current expected set.

## 8. The sequence at the GO (a procedure to be frozen, not performed)

1. **PREPARED**: every decision confirmed (including the order, Q6a), every pre-execution condition verified; the two storage locations exist and have been read back.
2. **The owner's GO**, recorded separately with the digest of the prepared state.
3. The manifest is built from the then-current commits, compared with the expected set and verified (`build_manifest.py`, `verify_manifest.py`); its digest is recorded.
4. One request per authority for **the same digest**; each raw token verified **offline** at its own signed time against an independently obtained trust anchor.
5. The raw tokens and the chains are placed in both locations **immediately** and read back (**EVIDENCE_CAPTURED**).
6. The package is completed, the third copy made, the restore drills done, the renewal planned, by the internal latest date (**PRESERVATION_COMPLETE**).

## 9. What this does not show

* **A recorded confirmation is a statement the check cannot authenticate** (section 1); the confirmation is not the GO and does not fulfil a condition.
* **No provider's terms, access or trust chain was verified**: the statements about DigiCert and GlobalSign are the reviewer's reading of their public documentation, not checked here; the public DigiCert endpoint's terms of use were not found on 2026-11-03.
* **The check proves the structure, the phases and the evidence links**, not that a condition is true in the world: a verified condition is as strong as the evidence file it names. For the conditions that need the tokens the evidence must be **digest-bound**; the reference conditions of the pre-execution phase are matched by a text, which proves the consistency of the reference and not the external fact.
* The dry-run manifest digest is not the manifest that will be time-stamped; the expected set is the inventory's snapshot of 2026-11-23.
* **The exact proof depends on the 92 records being all that matters**; the template releases and the three stamped releases are outside it, and the ended template releases cannot be repaired by any stamp (the expiry assessment).
